# Colorful Daily Fiber — Product and Brand Direction

Date: September 20, 2026  
Status: User-approved concept direction for development and validation.  
Market: United States, direct-to-consumer with optional recurring replenishment.  
Purpose: Internal planning brief, not final packaging copy or a manufacturing specification.

## The decision

Build a colorful, approachable digestive-wellness brand around one unflavored daily fiber powder. Combine a simple product, an enjoyable everyday routine, distinctive packaging and clear subscription terms.

The user approved this direction after reviewing the broader supplement research. Product performance, formulation, price and demand still need validation. The brand name has not been selected.

## Product and customer

| Element | Agreed direction |
|---|---|
| Hero product | One easy-mixing, unflavored fiber powder |
| Formulation candidate | PHGG, or partially hydrolyzed guar gum; final ingredient, dose and directions require formulation review |
| Initial range | One hero SKU, approximately 30 servings |
| Customer | Adults seeking a consistent fiber routine who dislike gritty textures, overly sweet drinks and complicated blends |
| Desired experience | Straightforward to measure, mix and fit into an existing routine |
| Primary pack | A distinctive countertop tub |
| Replenishment format | A simple refill pouch, subject to packaging and supplier feasibility |
| Price hypothesis | Around $29 for 30 servings; dependent on landed costs and willingness to pay |
| Subscription | Optional, with flexible delivery, clear renewal dates and easy skipping or cancellation |

Start by evaluating a single-fiber formula. Consider collagen, probiotics, electrolytes or other additions only if testing identifies a clear customer benefit. Ingredient combinations are not yet selected or approved.

Colorful branding does not require a colored or heavily flavored formula. The product can remain unflavored while the packaging supplies the personality. Mixing performance and compatibility with specific drinks or foods must be tested before making those promises.

## Brand personality and visual identity

The personality should be bright, playful and reassuring. Product information should be simple to find and easy to understand.

| Color | Intended role |
|---|---|
| Tangerine orange | Primary recognition color and focal accents |
| Lilac | Supporting color for packaging panels and digital backgrounds |
| Warm cream | Main neutral background and visual breathing room |
| Dark plum | Main text and clear information hierarchy |

Exact color values, typography and layout remain design decisions. Check readability and contrast in actual packaging and digital applications before finalizing them.

The tub should feel at home on a kitchen counter. Give the product name, ingredient, serving information and directions clear priority. Use the same visual system on the refill pouch so replenishment feels like part of the product experience.

## Positioning

The central communication idea is to make daily fiber easier to fit into an existing routine. This is a positioning direction, not finalized advertising copy.

The proposed combination is:

- A simple formula customers can understand.
- A use experience customers prefer over their current alternative.
- Colorful packaging they enjoy keeping visible.
- Clear guidance and predictable replenishment.

The visual identity can attract attention, but the product must earn repeat use. A new label on a generic powder is not enough to establish differentiation.

## Customer pain points to address

These themes come from the existing qualitative research. They are hypotheses to test with the intended audience, not measured prevalence or proven converting angles.

| Pain point | Product or communication response | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Gritty or unpleasant texture | Prioritize mixing and mouthfeel | Comparison with existing products at intended serving size |
| Drinks that taste too sweet | Evaluate an unflavored formula | Taste and aftertaste feedback |
| Too many ingredients and confusing blends | Make the ingredient and serving information clear | Customer comprehension testing |
| Concern about bloating or tolerance | Give accurate directions and realistic expectations | Appropriate product evaluation; no universal tolerance promise |
| Difficulty maintaining the routine | Fit use into an existing daily habit | Actual consumption and repeat-use observations |
| Excess stock or unexpected charges | Adjustable delivery and explicit renewal details | Pilot feedback and subscription behavior |

Do not turn discussion of IBS, medication side effects or other conditions into treatment claims. Terms such as “no bloating,” “gentle” or “clinically proven” require evidence appropriate to the actual finished product and claim.

## Offer and economics

Use $29 for 30 servings as the initial price to test, not a confirmed launch price. The earlier opportunity brief also proposes a $34 one-time purchase and 30/45/60-day delivery options; those remain offer hypotheses rather than finalized terms.

Obtain actual quotes for ingredients, manufacturing, tub, refill pouch, testing, fulfillment and shipping before committing. The existing economics model uses assumed costs and retention; it does not demonstrate profitable acquisition or recurring revenue.

## Competitive hurdle

Compare the concept with Hilma, Regular Girl, Momentous Fiber+ and an inexpensive fiber alternative. The research already identifies established products and lower-priced offers. Success requires a meaningful preference at the proposed price, supported by credible product information and a reliable use experience.

Do not claim to be the cheapest, uniquely gentle or the first simple fiber product without evidence. See the linked research report for dated offer snapshots and their verification limits.

## Validation before launch

1. Confirm the audience's current fiber products, frustrations and actual spending.
2. Review the candidate formulation and obtain comparable supplier quotes and quality documentation.
3. Test mixing, taste, mouthfeel and use experience against established alternatives.
4. Test willingness to pay with the real proposed offer and visible renewal terms.
5. Pilot one SKU and measure consumption, complaints, refunds and paid reorders before expanding.

Proceed only if customers prefer the experience and the economics work. If they do not, revise or stop the concept rather than adding ingredients solely to justify a higher price.

## Open decisions

- Brand name and availability checks.
- Final formula, dose, directions and substantiated claims.
- Exact palette, typography and packaging artwork.
- Manufacturer, minimum order, landed cost and lead time.
- Tub/refill feasibility and launch sequence.
- Final pricing and replenishment cadence.
